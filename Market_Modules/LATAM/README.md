# LATAM Power Markets

Latin America features some of the world's most hydro-dominant and interconnected electricity grids, shaped by distinct regional geographies, heavy reliance on renewable baseloads, and evolving wholesale spot markets (such as Colombia's SIN and regional SIEPAC integrations). 

The regional modules standardize heterogeneous regulatory frameworks, asset registries, and transmission topologies into modular, data-driven analytical pipelines.

---

## Regional Context & Major Markets

Latin America represents one of the most dynamic energy transition corridors globally, characterized by massive hydro baseloads, rapid non-conventional renewable energy (NCRE) expansion, and distinct wholesale market architectures:

* **Brazil (SIN):** The region's largest grid system, operated by ONS. Dominated by large-scale hydro, extensive long-distance HVDC corridors, and booming utility-scale solar/wind expansion in the Northeast.
* **Mexico (SIN / MEM):** A large-scale power market operated by CENACE, driven by industrial manufacturing demand, thermal/gas baseloads, and ongoing transmission capacity challenges.
* **Chile (SEN):** A pioneer in spot market unbundling operated by C协调 (CEN). Notable for extreme geographic dispersion, high solar penetration in the Atacama Desert, and zero-marginal-cost price volatility dynamics.
* **Colombia (SIN):** A hydro-dominant bid-based wholesale market (XM / UPME) subject to El Niño climate vulnerability, actively integrating large utility-scale solar pipelines through 2033.
* **Argentina (SADI):** A heavily thermal and hydro system managed by CAMMESA, facing structural transmission bottlenecks amidst expanding Patagonia wind and Cuyo solar resources.
